\subsection{Power Spectrum Fidelity}
\label{sec:eval-psd}

Point-wise error metrics such as PSNR do not capture whether a lossy
reconstruction preserves the statistical properties that downstream
cosmological analyses depend on. We therefore additionally evaluate
reconstruction quality in Fourier space via the isotropic power spectral
density (PSD) $P(k)$ of the baryon density field, computed with
\textsc{Gimlet2}~\cite{gimlet2}, the same MPI/FFTW-based analysis toolkit
used to post-process production Nyx simulation snapshots. \textsc{Gimlet2}
computes $P(k)$ by FFTing the overdensity field and isotropically binning
the result into log-spaced $k$-shells.

\paragraph{Setup.} We evaluate the NYX $512^3$ \texttt{baryon\_density}
field at a single operating point of effective compression ratio
$\mathrm{CR}\approx500$, comparing the \textbf{SZ3} base codec against
the two learned residual correctors applied on top of it --
\textbf{Ours} and the competing \textbf{NeurLZ}. To place all three at
the same compression ratio, SZ3's \texttt{rel\_err} is binary-searched to
land on $\mathrm{CR}=500$ ($\texttt{rel\_err}=1.13\times10^{-5}$); the
realized ratios span $480.9$--$508.0$, so no method is advantaged by
operating at a lower compression ratio. Both correctors receive the same
five raw auxiliary NYX fields as additional input channels (dark matter
density, temperature, and the three velocity components); these are
side-information and are not charged to the compression ratio. Ours is
trained for 10 epochs with per-operating-point Bayesian optimization of
learning rate and slice axis; NeurLZ is given an equal wall-clock
training budget ($\approx26\,\mathrm{s}$, 18 epochs) rather than a fixed
epoch count.

For each reconstruction, we build an HDF5 snapshot that is byte-identical
to the original -- same grid size, same cosmology metadata -- except that
the \texttt{baryon\_density} values are replaced with that method's
reconstruction. Because \textsc{Gimlet2} determines its $k$-bin edges
from the grid geometry alone (not from the field values), this guarantees
all four power spectra (original plus three reconstructions) fall into
exactly the same $k$-bins, so a direct, bin-by-bin comparison is valid.

\paragraph{Metric.} For each $k$-bin we report the absolute relative
error
\[
  \varepsilon(k) \;=\; \left| \frac{P_{\text{method}}(k) - P_{\text{orig}}(k)}{P_{\text{orig}}(k)} \right|,
\]
restricted to $k < 10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, the large- to
intermediate-scale range where Nyx analyses (e.g., Lyman-$\alpha$ forest
statistics) conventionally require $P(k)$ to be converged to within
\textbf{1\%}~\cite{lukic2015nyx} for downstream results to be trustworthy.

\paragraph{Results.} Figure~\ref{fig:psd} plots $\varepsilon(k)$ over the
18 bins with $k<10$; Table~\ref{tab:psd-relerr} gives the aggregates.

\textbf{SZ3+Ours} is the most faithful reconstruction, with a mean error
of $0.122\%$ and a worst case of $0.510\%$ -- a $4.6\times$ reduction in
mean error over its own SZ3 base, achieved together with an
$11.6\,\mathrm{dB}$ PSNR gain. It is the only SZ3-derived method that
meets the Nyx 1\% requirement at every bin: bare \textbf{SZ3} violates it
at the two largest scales ($2.39\%$ at $k=0.53$, $2.68\%$ at $k=0.97$),
precisely the regime that matters most for large-volume cosmological
statistics. \textbf{SZ3+NeurLZ} is visually almost indistinguishable from
the SZ3 base in Figure~\ref{fig:psd}: at an equal wall-clock budget it
recovers no PSNR at all ($+0.0\,\mathrm{dB}$) and, while its mean
$\varepsilon(k)$ is marginally lower than SZ3's ($0.512\%$ vs.\
$0.565\%$), it still breaches the 1\% requirement at the same two
large-scale bins ($2.08\%$ and $2.32\%$).

Notably, the $P(k)$ ranking is not implied by PSNR. SZ3 and SZ3+NeurLZ
reach the same PSNR to within $0.1\,\mathrm{dB}$, yet differ in mean
$\varepsilon(k)$; more importantly, a method can lower total squared
error while leaving it concentrated at the large scales where $P(k)$ is
most sensitive and where few modes are available to average it down. PSNR
integrates over all scales and cannot express that distinction, so it is
an inadequate acceptance criterion on its own -- which is precisely why
we report $P(k)$ fidelity alongside it.
